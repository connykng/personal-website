Hi, I'm Conny — a PhD candidate in Philosophy at the University of Pittsburgh, advised by Bob Batterman. My dissertation asks how scientists can make use of AI models they can't fully see inside of.

📂 Research.dir

My interests are in general philosophy of science, the philosophy of AI, and the philosophy and history of mathematics. More specifically, I work on questions about AI models used in science, the issue of generalization in deep learning, and trust and values in science.

🗂️ Dissertation.dir

"DLMs in Science: Epistemic Legitimacy without Representation"

In my dissertation, I argue that deep learning models (DLMs) pose particular epistemic risks given their opacity, and that these risks threaten their legitimate deployment in science. However, I argue that the literature commonly misunderstands this issue by treating them like scientific models and demanding that they become fine-grained representational models. I argue that their epistemic profile is better understood in comparison to scientific instruments. Opacity, so understood, is not a failure of representation but a challenge to validation. I propose a framework of external validation that enables us to assess whether the patterns a DLM exploits track the phenomenon of interest versus an artifact in the training data. An important claim in my thesis is that internal transparency and representation is not necessary for a DLM to contribute to scientific explanations.

My dissertation consists of four chapters: (1) The Legitimacy Gap, a three-level opacity analysis; (2) Against Representation, a critique of DLMs-as-representational-scientific-models; (3) A Diagnostic Taxonomy of DLM deployments; (4) Workflow-Conferred Legitimacy, my positive account.
